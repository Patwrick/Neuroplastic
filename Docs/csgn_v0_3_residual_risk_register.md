# CSGN v0.3 Residual Risk Register
## Problems not solved merely by adopting the revised equations

**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**Status:** Open design and experimental requirements  
**Related:** `Docs/csgn_paper_v0_3.md`, `Docs/csgn_math_feasibility_audit_v0_3.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

CSGN v0.3 repairs several deterministic defects in v0.2, but it does not prove that continual learning, credit assignment, replay, routing, or structural adaptation will work. This register identifies remaining risks that could still invalidate the architecture even after the revised equations are implemented correctly.

---

## 2. Transactional state migration

A sleep candidate can preserve effective edge values at the start of maintenance and still invalidate the agent's live recurrent state after replay optimization. The live hidden state, working memory, router caches, eligibility traces, and episodic-query context were generated by the pre-sleep dynamics.

A commit must therefore choose one of the following:

1. **State reconstruction:** retain a recent trusted context window and recompute recurrent and working-memory state under the accepted model.
2. **State adapter:** train and validate a migration map from old state to new state.
3. **State-preserving optimization:** constrain the candidate strongly enough that live-state outputs remain within tolerance, then validate on the exact live state.
4. **Episode-boundary commit:** permit major commits only at an explicit reset boundary.

The default should be state reconstruction. A model update must not be committed while silently retaining an incompatible hidden state.

---

## 3. Concurrent wake and asynchronous sleep

If wake operation continues while a shadow sleep transaction is trained, new fast writes and episodic records arrive after the transaction snapshot. A naïve commit loses or misapplies those updates.

The maintenance controller needs multiversion semantics:

- snapshot model version \(v\);
- append post-snapshot wake events to a versioned delta log;
- validate the candidate against version \(v\);
- pause commits briefly;
- rebase or replay the delta log onto the candidate;
- revalidate affected anchors and the live state;
- atomically publish version \(v+1\), or abort.

Every fast trace, eligibility record, episodic key, topology proposal, and replay sample must carry the model/topology version under which it was produced.

---

## 4. Snapshot and rollback cost

At large scale, keeping a complete old model, shadow candidate, optimizer state, and rollback copy can multiply storage and bandwidth. Transactional sleep is feasible only with:

- copy-on-write blocks;
- immutable cold segments;
- compact update journals;
- checksummed manifests;
- incremental checkpoints;
- bounded retention of historical versions;
- tested crash recovery.

The systems benchmark must include snapshot bytes, journal bytes, validation reads, and rollback time. These costs cannot be omitted from the scale claim.

---

## 5. Anchor-set incompleteness

Passing a finite anchor set does not guarantee that old knowledge was preserved. A candidate can overfit anchors while damaging unprobed behavior.

Required mitigations include:

- hidden holdout anchors unavailable to the adapting model;
- rotating and stratified anchor cohorts;
- trajectory-level anchors rather than isolated inputs;
- behavioral property tests and adversarial probes;
- uncertainty-aware rejection when coverage is weak;
- periodic full evaluations independent of the sleep controller.

Anchor validation is a safety filter, not a proof of global non-forgetting.

---

## 6. Meta-training distribution failure

The plasticity rule, router, and sleep policy may work only on the task family used for meta-training. Out-of-distribution operation can produce plasticity collapse, saturation, incorrect modulation, or routing failure.

Experiments must include held-out task families, longer horizons than training, unseen delay distributions, changed observation statistics, and altered reward structure. Meta-generalization is a central hypothesis, not an assumption.

---

## 7. Plasticity collapse and loss of plasticity

Optimization can discover two degenerate strategies:

- set plasticity near zero to protect old behavior;
- keep plasticity high and use fast state as an unstable shortcut.

Long training can also reduce the network's ability to learn new tasks. CSGN therefore requires reserve plastic capacity, saturation metrics, controlled resets or reinitialization experiments, and explicit regularizers for both adaptation and retention.

---

## 8. Long-horizon credit assignment

Bounded eligibility and regional modulation improve numerical stability but do not make the update an unbiased gradient of a long-horizon objective. Delayed rewards, partial observability, recurrent loops, and action-dependent observations can still assign credit incorrectly.

The architecture should be rejected for a domain if matched gradient-based, memory-based, or model-based baselines consistently dominate it. Multiple eligibility timescales, learned local error signals, and model-based counterfactual replay are experiments—not guaranteed solutions.

---

## 9. Reward and trust gaming

A protected controller can be immutable while still receiving manipulated inputs. The adaptive agent may learn actions that increase its reward, novelty, confidence, or trust signals without improving the intended objective.

Trust must combine external provenance, consistency checks, rate limits, independent monitors, and delayed verification. No model-generated confidence score should independently authorize a slow write.

---

## 10. Replay self-contamination

Generated or reconstructed memories can accumulate model errors across sleep cycles. Replaying those memories as fact creates a self-confirming feedback loop.

Every replay item must retain provenance and generation depth. Synthetic records should have lower consolidation authority, be checked against external evidence where possible, and be prevented from recursively promoting themselves to trusted ground truth.

---

## 11. Structural-search value

Candidate-set rewiring makes the computation finite, but rewiring may still add no value beyond a well-trained fixed sparse graph. It can also destabilize node semantics, invalidate caches, and consume disproportionate validation compute.

Structural plasticity remains optional until it beats fixed-topology and standard dynamic-sparse baselines under matched edge count, bandwidth, replay, and wall-clock budgets.

---

## 12. Sparse hardware reality

Irregular gather/scatter, poor locality, routing metadata, and synchronization can dominate arithmetic. A model with low theoretical FLOPs can be slower and less energy-efficient than a denser structured baseline.

No scale-up decision should use parameter count or sparse FLOPs alone. Required measurements include effective memory bandwidth, cache hit rate, index bytes per useful multiply, kernel occupancy, communication volume, and end-to-end latency.

---

## 13. Finite memory and unavoidable forgetting

CSGN has finite slow, working, and episodic memory. It must eventually compress, merge, or discard information. The research objective is therefore an improved adaptation–retention frontier under a declared budget—not perfect lifelong retention.

Every evaluation must report memory growth, compaction loss, retrieval loss, and which information classes are intentionally forgotten.

---

## 14. Minimum commit protocol

```text
1. Freeze snapshot version v and create copy-on-write manifest.
2. Log all later wake events against v.
3. Transfer selected fast state into slow state without changing effective weights.
4. Train shadow candidate and bounded topology proposal.
5. Validate anchors, live-state probes, recurrent stability, safety, and resources.
6. Rebase post-snapshot wake deltas and reconstruct live recurrent/WM state.
7. Re-run affected validation tests.
8. Atomically publish v+1 only if all hard checks pass.
9. Otherwise discard candidate and retain v.
10. Preserve an auditable journal of the decision.
```

---

## 15. Final residual verdict

The revised architecture is technically testable, but the decisive uncertainties remain empirical: meta-generalization, long-horizon credit assignment, replay quality, structural value, poisoning resistance, and sparse-system efficiency. None should be treated as solved by the mathematical bounds alone.

**End of register.**
